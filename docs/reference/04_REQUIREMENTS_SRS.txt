## 1. Purpose of This Document

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines what the Heera Organics customer shopping application and its authorized internal dairy operations interface must do, who they serve, the business rules they must follow, and how completion will be verified. It is the requirements baseline for the Waterfall lifecycle and should be approved before detailed system design and implementation begin.

This document defines **what the product must do**. The companion `02_Heera_Organics_System_Design.md` proposes **how the product and its technical components should be structured**.

### 1.1 Intended readers

- Business owner and decision-makers
- UI/UX designer using Google Stitch
- Frontend/mobile application developer
- Odoo implementer or backend developer
- QA/test engineer
- Hosting and operations provider
- Payment, OTP, delivery and other integration providers, when selected

### 1.2 Requirement wording

- **MUST** means required for the agreed release.
- **SHOULD** means strongly recommended; any exception must be recorded.
- **MAY** means optional or dependent on a future decision.
- **TBD** means the business or technical owner must decide before the affected requirement can be finalized.

## 2. Product Overview

Heera Organics is a B2C local grocery and dairy business focused on responsibly sourced food, freshness, convenient online ordering, and a farm-to-family experience. The customer application will allow people in eligible delivery areas to discover products, search and browse the catalogue, maintain a cart, place orders, and view order information. An internal dairy operations interface will support authorized staff with daily milk production/collection logs, customer-wise milk delivery records, delivery-route planning and route completion. Both interfaces should use one approved operational backend, with Odoo Community as the current working assumption.

The brand's primary market is expected to begin in Kalaburagi and eligible nearby areas. The business may expand to more locations and physical retail outlets over time. The initial release should therefore support configurable delivery coverage rather than permanently hard-coding a single city.

### 2.1 Product goals

1. Make online grocery shopping convenient and easy to understand.
2. Help customers discover available groceries, produce, and dairy products.
3. Build trust through accurate product information, sourcing transparency, and honest claims.
4. Check delivery availability early and validate the delivery address before checkout.
5. Use Odoo as the main operational source for catalogue, pricing, stock, customers, and orders, subject to the modules and customizations confirmed for the project.
6. Establish a foundation that can expand to more delivery areas and physical retail operations.
7. Give authorized dairy staff accurate, auditable milk logs and route-level delivery visibility without creating a separate conflicting source of truth.

### 2.2 Target customers

- Working professionals, approximately 22–45 years old
- Families seeking convenient grocery shopping
- Customers who value freshness, sourcing information, and responsible farming practices
- Customers ordering daily or weekly household essentials

### 2.3 Brand positioning

**Tagline:** Naturally Grown, Honestly Delivered.  
**Brand promise:** From Our Farm to Your Family.

The app should communicate a natural, honest, warm, family-focused, and convenient brand experience. Product-specific organic/certification claims must be shown only when supported by approved evidence.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope for the customer application

- App launch/welcome experience
- Location permission, manual location/pincode entry, and serviceability checks
- Mobile-number and OTP authentication, subject to selected provider and agreed guest-browsing policy
- Home page with animated hero, category grid, value propositions, promotional cards/banners, horizontal product sliders, curated collections, farm story, comparison section, testimonials, FAQ preview, and footer/support entry points as applicable to the selected app format
- Product categories, subcategories, category browsing, and product listings
- Product search, suggestions, filters, and sorting
- Product detail pages and quick-view sheets
- Cart and server-revalidated totals
- Checkout, delivery address, enabled delivery options, payment selection, and order confirmation
- Customer profile, saved addresses, order history, order details, and supported tracking/status views
- Wishlist, if enabled for the initial release
- Help, FAQs, support entry points, and chatbot entry point
- Referral/rewards screens only if the programme is configured and approved
- Loading, empty, error, retry, unavailable, and network-failure states
- Integration with Odoo through secure API endpoints/custom HTTP controllers implemented in approved Odoo modules; no separate FastAPI business backend in the target architecture
- Responsive layouts and accessibility considerations
- Basic operational logging and error reporting for application support

### 3.2 In scope for backend configuration/integration

- Product and category data
- Prices, units, variants, and stock/availability
- Customer identity/profile mapping
- Saved delivery addresses where supported
- Cart/order lifecycle and order history
- Promotions and coupons, if configured
- Delivery zones, serviceability, slots, and ETA, if supported by the configured system
- Payment status integration with a selected payment provider
- Customer support configuration and product/content metadata

### 3.3 In scope for the internal dairy operations interface

- Daily milk production entries by date and shift (morning/evening), keeping cow and buffalo milk quantities separate.
- Customer registration through owner/admin-generated invitation links, including required GPS capture, and customer/driver assignment management.
- Customer regular daily cow/buffalo quantities, date-specific extra-milk quantities, and date-specific delivery pauses. Regular daily-quantity changes are owner-only; other agreed administrative actions follow the role matrix below.
- Daily route assignment, automatic shortest-driving-route stop ordering, temporary full-route reassignment, and route progress/completion.
- Delivery-boy actions: Delivered, Partial, Skip Cow, Skip Buffalo and Skip Both; actual quantity capture for partial delivery; exact target loading checks before route start; and actual returned milk quantities at route end.
- Offline capture of production and delivery records, visible sync state, retry, duplicate prevention, and app-admin alerts for sync failures.
- An owner/admin audit-history view and Excel exports of production, delivery, reconciliation and audit reports.
- Integration with Odoo through approved APIs/custom modules; Odoo owns the official records and calculations.
- Reuse of the existing interface at https://dairy-farm-w8p6.vercel.app as a workflow/reference input, subject to code review and Odoo integration.

**Dairy app boundary:** The dairy app is for daily milk logging and daily delivery operations, including customer registration, assignments, route execution, corrections and their operational reports. Overall inventory management, milk usage for product manufacturing, wastage, closing-stock accounting, billing, product sales, herd management and other business operations are handled in Odoo, not as separate dairy-app modules. The app may capture operational facts that Odoo needs, but it must not create an independent inventory/billing system.

### 3.4 Out of scope unless separately approved

- A full herd-management system covering veterinary care, breeding, animal records and feed formulation
- A separate full-featured retailer/POS application
- A new inventory-management system duplicating Odoo
- Building a custom payment gateway or SMS carrier
- Guaranteed delivery in ten minutes or any other unverified SLA
- Unconfigured loyalty, cashback, gift, referral, subscription, or recurring-order programmes
- Medical, nutritional, or disease-prevention advice
- Certification claims without documentary substantiation
- Multi-vendor marketplace settlement, unless the business model is formally expanded
- Advanced demand forecasting or AI-powered product recommendations for the initial release

### 3.5 Existing dairy repository baseline (As-Is)

A read-only source review of [the existing dairy-farm repository](https://github.com/shubhamsh1355-collab/dairy-farm) found an existing prototype with the following stack and workflows:

- **Frontend:** Expo / React Native with TypeScript and Expo Router; web export is also configured.
- **Backend:** FastAPI / Python.
- **Database:** MongoDB accessed through Motor/PyMongo.
- **Existing workflows:** daily dashboard, cow/buffalo milk log, shift-based production entry, milk inventory, dairy-product inventory and sales, customer records and daily requirements, delivery-staff management, assigned customer route list, delivery outcomes, skips/monthly billing, analytics, herd records, invitations and WhatsApp broadcast flow.

This describes the current codebase only. It does **not** mean these features are production-ready, fully tested, or connected to Odoo. Many as-is screens (product inventory/sales, herd management, billing/analytics and broadcast tools) are not part of the dairy app's agreed responsibility in the target state; their applicable business data and back-office operations belong in Odoo. The reviewed source contains prototype authentication and data-integrity concerns that must be resolved before real customer/business data is used. See `Dairy_Farm_Repository_Audit.md` for the source review and findings.

**Approved target decision:** Odoo is the only business backend and operational database system for the integrated solution. Odoo uses its supported PostgreSQL database internally; neither customer nor dairy clients connect directly to PostgreSQL. The current FastAPI/MongoDB implementation is legacy-only. MongoDB may be accessed only for a controlled, one-time export/migration and approved backup/retention period. It must not remain an active target datastore after cutover. FastAPI will not be deployed as a separate target backend; required app-specific APIs and business logic will be implemented in approved Odoo modules/controllers.

### 3.6 Platform assumption to confirm


The design is mobile-first. The current direction is a Next.js/TypeScript customer frontend, the existing Expo/React Native + TypeScript dairy frontend, and Odoo Community as the sole business backend and operational data store. Secure app-specific API endpoints will be implemented inside Odoo through approved modules/controllers; no separate FastAPI service or MongoDB database is part of the target architecture. The business must confirm the release platform for the customer app and the internal operations interface. For the customer app, confirm whether release 1 is:

1. a responsive web application/PWA;
2. a web application packaged for iOS/Android distribution; or
3. a separately implemented cross-platform/native mobile app.

This choice affects authentication, push notifications, app-store packaging, deep links, and device integration. The internal operations interface must also be confirmed as a responsive web app/PWA or another approved client. Business direction is to retain the existing Expo/React Native frontend as the starting point, subject to UX/security review and Odoo integration work. Both clients must use Odoo and consistent server-side authorization. Platform packaging and detailed implementation choices must be resolved before build sign-off.

## 4. Stakeholders and User Classes

| Stakeholder / class | Main needs | Access boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Guest visitor | Browse available products and understand delivery coverage | Public catalogue and public content only, if guest browsing is enabled |
| Registered customer | Search, cart, checkout, addresses, orders, support | Their own account and orders only |
| Customer support staff | Resolve order and delivery issues | Access through authorized Odoo/support tools; no unrestricted app-client access |
| Owner | Full control of the dairy app; sole role allowed to change a customer's regular daily milk quantities; can review all operational records and reports | Full owner permissions |
| Dairy app admin | Manage daily operations, customer registration/assignments, extra-milk schedules, delivery pauses, user accounts/roles, corrections, reports and alerts | Admin permissions; cannot change regular daily milk quantities |
| Delivery staff/driver | Use PIN login, view assigned route and necessary stop details, record delivery actions, actual loaded/returned quantities, and correct their own submitted delivery records | Assigned routes/stops only; no customer phone number or full delivery history |
| Grocery customer | Browse/order groceries in the customer app | Own account and orders only; dairy delivery history is not exposed |
| Application developer/operator | Maintain app integration, deployment and diagnostics | Environment-specific least-privilege access |
| External providers | OTP, payment, mapping, analytics, AI support, where selected | Only the specific data and actions required for the integration |

The customer app must never expose Odoo administrator credentials or allow a customer to access another customer's information.

#### Customer registration and requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-CREG-01 | The owner/admin MUST be able to generate and share a customer registration link. The link remains valid until successful registration; after success it cannot register another customer and displays **“You have already registered.”** | Must | Reopening a completed link shows the specified message; an unused link remains usable. |
| FR-CREG-02 | Registration MUST collect the customer's name, phone number, required daily cow-milk litres and required daily buffalo-milk litres, then capture GPS coordinates. #### Extra milk and route assignment

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-EXTRA-01 | A customer must request extra milk at least one day before it is needed. The owner/admin then schedules the one-time extra cow/buffalo milk litres by selecting one or more delivery dates. Extra quantities apply only to the selected dates and do not alter regular daily requirements. | Must | Each selected date has its own approved target; same-day new requests are not accepted; regular quantity remains unchanged on other dates. |
| FR-EXTRA-02 | The owner/admin MUST be able to edit or cancel a scheduled extra-milk request before its route starts. Once the route starts, that route's extra-milk request and target are locked. | Must | Edits/cancellations are blocked after route start; changes before route start update the target. The customer receives WhatsApp and SMS notifications when an extra-milk request is scheduled, changed or cancelled. |
| FR-ROUTE-11 | The owner/admin MUST be able to temporarily reassign an entire day's route to another delivery boy for a defined date range. The temporary assignment expires automatically and the regular assignment resumes; the permanent assignment and audit history are preserved. | Must | A replacement can see the route only for the approved period; the normal driver returns automatically after the period. Upcoming routes must be reassigned before a delivery-boy account is deactivated. |
| FR-ROUTE-12 | Before starting a route, the delivery boy MUST enter actual cow-milk and buffalo-milk litres loaded. The quantities must exactly match the approved target load for each type, including any approved extra-milk requests; unapproved excess or a shortage blocks route start. No safety-buffer milk is loaded. | Must | Route start remains disabled until both values are entered and match the target; any shortage/excess must be resolved at the farm. |
| FR-ROUTE-13 | At route end, the delivery boy MUST enter actual cow-milk and buffalo-milk litres returned to the farm. | FR-ROUTE-14 | When a stop is skipped, the app MUST save the selected skip and reason, then guide the driver to the next best pending stop. The app MUST preserve the five existing delivery actions: Delivered, Partial, Skip Cow, Skip Buffalo and Skip Both. | Must | Skip state/reason are retained, route progress updates, and navigation is recalculated. |

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-SYNC-01 | Milk production logs and delivery records MUST be capturable offline. | FR-MILK-09 | Odoo MUST provide a consolidated daily milk reconciliation view showing morning/evening production, total produced, actual customer deliveries, milk used for products, wastage, calculated balance, physical closing-stock counts, discrepancy, unsynced records and exceptions. Both owner and admin may correct authorized records from any prior date; a delivery boy may correct only their own submitted delivery record. | Must | Audit history displays the before/after values and metadata; there is no permanent-delete action. |
| FR-AUD-02 | The dairy app MUST provide an Audit History screen for owner/admin. Audit entries themselves cannot be edited or deleted. | Must | Authorized reviewers can filter/review changes and access is denied to delivery boys. |
| FR-REP-01 | Owner/admin MUST be able to export milk production, delivery, reconciliation and audit-history reports to Excel, filtering by date range, delivery boy, customer and milk type (cow/buffalo). | Must | Exported records match the applied filters and authoritative Odoo data. |

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |

### Data-quality requirements

- Every product/variant must have a stable identifier.
- Prices must include explicit currency and selling unit/pack size.
- Availability must have a clear meaning (for example, available/unavailable); do not infer exact stock counts unless exposed intentionally.
- Image, description, storage, ingredients and certification fields may be optional; missing fields should not be fabricated.
- Customer addresses and coordinates must be stored/processed only for legitimate delivery purposes with appropriate access controls.
- Order totals must reconcile against the authoritative backend and payment provider.
- Milk quantities must be numeric, unit-labelled and associated with a valid date/shift.
- Milk log corrections and route-stop status changes must be attributable to an authorized staff user.
- Planned delivery quantities, actual delivery quantities, actual loaded quantities, actual returned quantities and production/collection quantities must be stored separately so reports do not conflate them.
- All cow/buffalo production, load, delivery, return, usage, wastage, calculated-balance and physical-count values must be kept separate; totals are derived.
- Offline submissions require stable idempotency identifiers, and sync state/error metadata must not be treated as business quantity data.

## 12. Error and Edge-Case Requirements

The app must handle at minimum:

- Location permission denied, GPS unavailable, invalid pincode, or area not served
- OTP incorrect, expired, resend not available, rate limited, or provider unavailable
- Search with no results or failed search request
- Empty category/product list
- Product becoming unavailable after it is displayed
- Product price/offer changing before checkout
- Network loss during cart update, checkout or order submission
- Payment success with delayed order confirmation
- Payment failure or cancellation
- Duplicate order-submit action
- Out-of-stock or invalid cart line at checkout
- Delivery address edited to a non-serviceable area
- Missing optional product information/images
- Expired/invalid promotion
- Unauthorized access to profile/order/address endpoints
- Chatbot/support provider unavailable
- Staff user attempts to access an unassigned delivery route
- Duplicate route-stop completion submission
- Milk log has missing date/shift, invalid unit or negative quantity
- Actual delivered milk quantity differs from planned quantity
- Route contains an invalid, inactive or non-deliverable customer/address
- Map/navigation provider is unavailable while manual route order is still needed

Each error state should explain what happened in simple language, preserve user input where safe, and show an appropriate recovery action. The app must not expose stack traces, internal hostnames or credentials.

## 13. Acceptance and Release Criteria

The initial release is acceptable only when all Must requirements are implemented and verified, known exceptions are approved, and the business signs off on the unresolved decisions that affect release.

### 13.1 Core end-to-end acceptance scenarios

1. A customer can set a serviceable location and reach the catalogue.
2. A customer denied GPS permission can enter a pincode/address manually.
3. A customer can authenticate through the approved OTP flow, including error and retry cases.
4. A customer can browse categories, search products, filter and sort supported results.
5. A customer can open a product, select an available variant and add it to the cart.
6. A customer can update quantities/remove items and see server-validated totals.
7. A customer can enter/select an address and see only supported delivery options and payment methods.
8. An order is confirmed only after backend confirmation; payment/order failure is handled without a misleading success state.
9. An authenticated customer can see only their own orders and addresses.
10. A customer can recover from empty results, empty cart and network/API errors.
11. Home page promotions, hero messages, product sliders and trust claims render accurately and link to correct destinations.
12. The interface is usable on the agreed target screen sizes and passes the agreed accessibility review.
13. No secret or administrator credential is present in the client application bundle.
14. Odoo and external integrations are tested in a non-production environment before production cutover.
15. Authorized staff can create, view and correct date/shift-based milk production/collection logs, and the displayed totals match the saved entries.
16. Staff can create a milk delivery route, order stops, assign it to a delivery staff member and see the same route plan in the authorized driver view.
17. Delivery staff can record delivered, partial, skipped and failed outcomes with actual quantities and required exception reasons.
18. The route summary reconciles completed/remaining stops and planned-versus-actual quantities; production/collection-versus-delivery comparison is shown only when both data sets exist.
19. Unassigned staff cannot read or change route stops or operational records outside their permissions; changes have an audit trail.
20. Production authentication never returns a real OTP to the client, and production startup fails if required signing secrets are missing.
21. Cross-farm tests prove that drivers cannot access or modify another farm's customers, skips, deliveries or milk inventory.
22. Backend validation rejects negative quantities, invalid milk types/shifts/statuses, and invalid partial-delivery quantities.
23. Daily and monthly milk/stock figures reconcile from the approved canonical record model, including shift production and customer deliveries.

### 13.2 Release blockers

- Any unresolved critical security/privacy issue
- Incorrect order totals or customer-data exposure
- Duplicate order/payment risk without a mitigation
- A path that allows placing an order for an unserviceable delivery address
- False order confirmation or false payment-success messaging
- Unapproved material business claims or invalid promotions
- Missing production policies/contact details required for launch
- Unverified production backups and restore procedure
- Milk logs or route completion can be modified without authorized access or an audit trail
- Delivery route totals/statuses do not reconcile with individual stop records

After the SRS baseline is approved, changes to scope or requirements must be recorded as a change request containing the reason, affected requirement IDs, impact on design/code/tests, schedule/cost impact and approval. Update the SRS, System Design Document, traceability matrix and test cases together. Do not silently change signed-off requirements during implementation.

## 15.1 Approved Architectural Direction (Business Decision)

The business has approved the following direction for planning purposes:

1. Retain the existing Expo/React Native + TypeScript dairy frontend as the starting point; improve it only where the audit, brand requirements, usability or integration work requires changes.
2. Use Odoo as the sole business backend and operational system of record for both the customer grocery app and dairy operations app.
3. Implement required app-specific endpoints and business rules inside approved Odoo modules/custom controllers. Do not deploy a separate FastAPI business backend.
4. Do not use MongoDB in the target solution. Treat the current MongoDB database only as a temporary legacy source for controlled data export/migration and a restricted backup/archive during an approved retention period. Disable legacy operational writes after cutover and decommission the legacy service when its retention/rollback window closes.
5. Odoo's own supported PostgreSQL database is the persistent database for the target system. Neither client connects to PostgreSQL directly.
6. Do not switch production traffic or decommission the legacy environment until backups, trial migration, reconciliation, acceptance testing and a rollback plan are approved.

This decision approves the destination and direction, not the implementation details. Odoo version/modules, model mapping, the API adapter, migration mechanics, and release gates remain subject to technical sign-off.
